A decade of production AWS

Scale, compliance, and platforms other teams build on.

$10M+/yr
of production AWS

At Protectwise, acquired by Verizon, I administered the AWS environment behind a petabyte-scale ingestion platform. Thousands of Cassandra nodes, petabytes in S3, and every piece of it managed as infrastructure as code.

4 regimes
HIPAA · PCI · SOC 2 · ISO 27001

HIPAA-compliant patient-care infrastructure at Inception Health, a PCI-compliant payments installation at Cardfree, and a multi-account model at Cloaked that met SOC 2, ISO 27001, and ISO 27701 requirements. Auditors have signed off on my architecture in three different industries.

CDK
as a paved road for other teams

At Inception Health I designed the CDK patterns that application teams consumed to ship HIPAA-compliant microservices themselves, collaborated directly with AWS partner teams, and trained the product and mobile teams until they ran their own deploys.

30x
faster deploys after the EKS migration

At Cloaked I led the move from a legacy PaaS onto AWS EKS. The new platform was cheaper, more scalable, and paired with a GitOps pipeline that accelerated deployments 30x in my first 6 months.

For the keyword scanners: CDK, Terraform, Chef, EKS, S3, Cassandra, Kafka, multi-account AWS, GitHub Actions, Tailscale, Cloudflare. Every one of them attached to a company above or on the resume.

The day-2 receipts

Build with costs in mind

It’s easy for costs to run away when nobody considers them at build time. I migrated Cloaked from a legacy PaaS onto EKS, and the bill went down while the headroom went up.

Petabytes, every day, for years

At Protectwise the ingestion never stopped. Keeping thousands of Cassandra nodes and the Kafka pipelines between them healthy was the everyday work, and the platform held through an acquisition.

Audit season, every year

HIPAA, PCI, SOC 2, and ISO audits recur, and mine kept passing. Account boundaries and controls built into the platform make each one evidence collection rather than a fire drill.
