Reliability with receipts

Scale, visibility, and what happens when the pager goes off.

Petabytes
at Protectwise, acquired by Verizon

Thousands of Cassandra nodes, petabytes in S3, and a $10M+/yr AWS environment administered entirely as infrastructure as code, through dozens of production deploys a day, underneath a product that could not miss data.

Observability
the whole company reads

Prometheus, Grafana, Loki, PagerDuty, and CloudWatch for the infrastructure. At Cloaked I built the product-analytics stack the whole company used to make product and roadmap calls.

IR program
PCI-compliant, paperwork-light

At Cardfree I replaced ad-hoc Slack alerts with a structured, PCI-compliant incident-response program. It satisfied the auditors without burying the engineers in paperwork.

On call
for my own house

My homelab serves production traffic from a k3s cluster with Prometheus, Grafana, and Loki, and Prometheus pages my phone when something breaks.

For the keyword scanners: Prometheus, Grafana, Loki, PagerDuty, CloudWatch, Cassandra, Kafka, S3, EKS, k3s, Flux CD, Terraform, Chef. Every one of them attached to a system above or on the resume.

The day-2 receipts

Deploy risk, engineered away

At CyberGRX I wrote a Go operator that ran blue/green rollouts as a custom resource. A bad deploy meant flipping back, and deploys went from a quarterly event to whenever anyone wanted.

Capacity ahead of the curve

The Cloaked platform carried the business from hundreds of users to hundreds of thousands without a rebuild. I was the one-person ops team behind it, handling capacity and cost under three compliance regimes.

The quiet pager

On my homelab, certs renew themselves, backups run on schedule, and every change ships from git, so the alerts that reach my phone are the ones that need a human.

Alex Artigues, DevOps Engineer, Protectwise
